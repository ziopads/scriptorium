## 1. Purpose

Scriptorium serves one reader working one reading list toward doctoral
comprehensive exams, and then serves the dissertation that follows from them. It
holds the books of the list as text, keeps a correct bibliographic record for
each, and exposes both to search, to retrieval, and to a conversational
assistant reached over the Model Context Protocol (MCP) — the interface through
which an assistant calls external tools.

It is a reading instrument. It does not write anything that goes into the
dissertation.

## 2. Users

**the reader** — doctoral candidate, Spanish & Portuguese, the university.
The reader. Works in Chicago 17th edition and has named citation accuracy and
citation auditing as an explicit priority in her own practice. Should be able to
use every part of this without a terminal.

**James Haskins** — builds and operates it. Runs ingest, extraction, chunking,
and embedding. Whether operation stays with him permanently is open (§8.6).

## 3. The corpus as it stands

Measured from `Listas_de_examenes_the reader_Octubre_2026.docx`, 8 September 2026.

| List | Entries |
|---|---|
| I. Theory | 25 |
| II. Dissertation | 20 |
| III. Teaching, Siglo XX | 35 |
| IV. Filmografía | 1 paired entry |
| **Total bullets** | **80** |

The handoff assumed roughly 100 books. It is 80 bullets, and the real book count
sits in the high 80s because several bullets carry two works — Rulfo's *Pedro
Páramo* alongside *El llano en llamas*, Corky González alongside La Chrisx, Rael
in two volumes. The list is three lists, which the `exam_lists` and `list_items`
tables already anticipate.

Bibliographic completeness is uneven. Around a dozen entries carry no publisher
or year. One carries the reader's own query, `[?] confirmar editorial`, on
Edmundo Delgado's *Witch Stories of New Mexico*. At least one attribution is
wrong: *Along the Archival Grain* is Ann Laura Stoler, and the list has "Soler,
Ana Laura." Bibliographic entry at ingest is therefore partly a correction pass,
not a transcription pass.

The corpus is bilingual throughout, and individual volumes are internally
bilingual — the New Mexico folklore collections routinely print Spanish and
English on facing pages. Language handling is a requirement of the substrate
rather than a refinement of it.

## 4. User stories

Identifiers are stable and may be cited in commits and in later documents.

### 4.0 Two horizons

**The design target is the dissertation.** She is reading the list now, defends
in the region of two years, and will use this against her own drafting for
longer than she will use it against the exam. Every schema decision is made for
that horizon.

**Comps are on the exam date**, thirty-four days from this draft. Whatever
lands by then is a gain. Two rules follow, and they matter more than any
sequencing:

1. Nothing is compromised to reach the date. No schema shortcut, no skipped
   `page_offset`, no bibliography entered fast and wrong. A record entered wrong
   in September is cited wrong for two years.
2. The comps slice is chosen so that failing to finish it costs nothing. Every
   story marked `Sept` below is useful on its own and is not a partial version
   of something else.

Stories that have to exist while she is still reading are marked `Sept`. The two
that could land by 12 October if the triage allows are marked `Oct`. Everything
unmarked belongs to the dissertation horizon. The marks are about *when the
story pays back*, not about difficulty.

### 4.1 What the comps slice is, and why

The two capabilities that help a reader who is *currently reading* are a correct
catalogue and a place to put notes. Neither requires a single page of text to be
extracted.

That is the whole argument for the slice. Extraction is the long pole and its
length is unknown until the scan-against-text-layer split is measured (§8.1). If
that measurement comes back badly, the catalogue and the notes still ship in
early October and she still gets thirty days of use out of them. If it comes
back well, position cards for the Theory list become reachable and are the most
direct exam preparation in the project.

Nothing in the comps slice is thrown away afterward. The catalogue is the
citation spine for everything above it, and notes taken in September attach to
books extracted in November (N-9).

### 4.2 Catalogue and bibliography

- **C-1** `Sept` — Enter each book once with Chicago-ready fields (author, title,
  subtitle, translator, editor, publisher, place, year, edition, language) and
  have every citation the system emits draw from that single record.
- **C-2** `Sept` — Record where a bibliographic field is unknown as unknown, and see
  at a glance which records are incomplete, so gaps are resolved deliberately
  rather than discovered in a footnote.
- **C-3** `Sept` — Establish the offset between printed page and file page once per
  book at ingest, and receive printed page numbers everywhere thereafter.
- **C-4** `Sept` — See the source condition of each book: text-layer PDF, scanned
  PDF put through optical character recognition (OCR), EPUB, or nothing held. An
  EPUB-sourced passage can be located but not cited, and the interface must say
  so at the point of use rather than in documentation.
- **C-5** `Sept` — Assign a book to one or more of the three lists, with a sentence
  of rationale per membership.
- **C-6** `Sept` — Mark a book unread, reading, or read.
- **C-7** — Link a book to its record in the Vivarium catalogue where one
  exists.

### 4.3 Notes and annotation

- **N-1** `Sept` — Attach a note to a book, optionally anchored to a printed page
  and a quoted passage.
- **N-2** — Write a note while reading and have the citation filled in from
  the passage, without leaving the note to look anything up.
- **N-3** `Sept` — Tag notes freely and retrieve by tag.
- **N-4** `Sept` — Retrieve notes by book, by tag, or by query, and see them beside
  retrieved passages from the same book.
- **N-5** — Ask the assistant to draft a note from a passage, then edit it.
  The draft is marked as assistant-originated and unreviewed until she has
  touched it.
- **N-6** `Sept` — Distinguish at a glance, and in export, between a note she wrote,
  a note she edited, and a note the assistant drafted and she has not yet read.
- **N-7** `Sept` — Export notes for a book or a tag with citations attached, in a
  form that can be pasted into a draft chapter.
- **N-8** — Have a note survive re-chunking of the corpus (§5.3).
- **N-9** `Sept` — Take a note on a book that has not been extracted yet, typing the
  page and the quotation by hand, and have that note attach cleanly when the book
  is extracted later. The quoted text is what re-locates it, so notes written in
  September are not orphaned by ingest in November.

### 4.4 Search and quotation verification

- **S-1** — Paste a passage she has slightly wrong and get back the book, the
  printed page, and the surrounding paragraph. The valuable case is the imperfect
  quotation; an exact-match-only tool reports nothing found and teaches her
  nothing.
- **S-2** — Search an exact phrase and have it matched with the correct
  language configuration, so that a Spanish query is not stemmed as English.
- **S-3** — Search unaccented and still match accented text, because OCR on
  Spanish scans mangles accented characters reliably.
- **S-4** — Restrict a search to bibliographies when the question is who
  cites whom, and exclude indexes always, because an index is noise in retrieval.
- **S-5** — Restrict a search to one list, or to one book.

### 4.5 Semantic retrieval

- **R-1** — Ask in English and receive relevant Spanish passages, and the
  reverse.
- **R-2** — Find passages that are conceptually related to a query and share
  no vocabulary with it.
- **R-3** — Widen the window around any hit to the pages on either side.
- **R-4** — See, for every returned passage, which book and which printed
  pages it came from, before deciding whether to read it.

### 4.6 Position cards and the field map

- **F-1** `Oct` — Hold one card per book: central claim in two
  sentences, method, key terms, what it argues against, and which dissertation
  chapter it feeds. Reachable before the exam only for books already extracted,
  and only after the card schema is settled with her (§8.4). The Theory list, at
  25 entries, is the sensible first target.
- **F-2** `Oct` — Review and correct every card. A card she has not
  checked is not exam preparation, and the interface tracks reviewed against
  unreviewed.
- **F-3** — Ask the assistant a question that requires comparing books across
  the whole list — which of these argue against each other, which share a method
  — and have it answered from all the cards at once rather than from retrieved
  fragments.
- **F-4** — Filter the field map to one list.

### 4.7 Operation

- **O-1** `Sept` — Add a book after the initial ingest without re-running anything
  for the other books. Required early, because books will arrive throughout
  September as she sources files.
- **O-2** — Change the chunking rules and rebuild one book's chunks in a
  transaction, leaving the extracted pages untouched.
- **O-3** — Tell, six weeks in, which books were chunked under which rules.
- **O-4** `Sept` — Read the extracted text of a page beside the source file, because
  every extraction failure mode is obvious that way and invisible in aggregate
  statistics.

## 5. Assistant-assisted annotation

The layer the handoff did not specify. It is the reason the MCP server exists
rather than a nicety on top of it.

### 5.1 Tool surface

| Tool | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|
| `field_map` | position cards, all or by list | — |
| `search` | chunks; modes keyword, semantic, hybrid | — |
| `verify_quotation` | chunks and pages | — |
| `expand_context` | pages | — |
| `get_book` | bibliography, card, notes | — |
| `get_notes` | notes, by book or query | — |
| `draft_note` | a passage | notes, marked and unreviewed |

`draft_note` is the only write tool. There is deliberately no tool to edit or
delete a note. Correction and deletion of her own writing stay in the
application, under her hand, where an accidental call cannot reach them.

### 5.2 Provenance

Every note carries an origin (`human` or `assistant`) and a reviewed flag,
mirroring `position_cards.reviewed`. A note written by `draft_note` is
`assistant` and `reviewed: false`. Editing it in the application sets
`reviewed: true` and leaves the origin unchanged, so the record of where a
sentence came from survives the edit.

The reason is not tidiness. She will eventually quote her own notes in a
dissertation, and a note whose provenance is unrecorded is a note she has to
re-verify against the source before she can use it. Recording origin once at
write time is cheaper than auditing later.

### 5.3 Anchoring

A note anchors to `(book_id, printed page, quoted text)`. It does not anchor to a
chunk.

This follows from the handoff's own division: pages are the durable artifact and
chunks are a derived cache, rebuilt whenever the boundary rules change, which
will happen at least twice. A note anchored to a chunk identifier is orphaned by
the first re-chunk. A note anchored to a page and a quotation survives, and can
be re-located in the new chunks by searching for its own quoted text. The same
property is what makes N-9 work.

### 5.4 Rate and scope of writing

`draft_note` writes one note per call and requires a `book_id` and a quoted
passage. It does not accept a list of notes. Bulk generation of notes across a
book is not a feature; the review burden it creates is the same burden the
position cards already impose, and doubling it defeats the point.

## 6. Non-functional requirements

- **Citation correctness outranks recall.** A passage returned with the wrong
  printed page is worse than a passage not returned.
- **Blank beats a guess.** Carried from Vivarium. An unknown publisher is empty,
  never inferred.
- **Every returned passage carries its book and its printed page range.** No
  retrieval result is ever unattributed, including inside assistant responses.
- **Extraction and chunking are deterministic and local.** No model call, no
  network, no key. Embedding is the only step that leaves the machine.
- **Extracted pages are held on disk as JSON, one file per book, and the
  database is derived from them.** The chunker reads files and writes files and
  is testable with no database at all.
- **The full text of eighty-odd copyrighted monographs sits in hosted
  infrastructure** (§8.2). Retrieved fragments reaching an assistant is ordinary
  use; the complete corpus in someone else's Postgres and object storage is a
  different exposure, accepted deliberately in exchange for an instrument she can
  reach from a phone and that can be repaired without a house call.
- **Simplicity is a hard requirement.** A workaround that papers over an
  underlying problem is removed rather than accommodated.
- **Staging is deliberate.** Files are named at `git add`; never `git add -A`.

## 7. Out of scope

- The dissertation's field material. *the dissertation*
  privileges oral tradition and lived community as primary sources. This tool
  serves the exam list, which is books.
- Page-level control numbers, redaction codes, and cross-production duplicate
  alignment. Those belong to Inquest and its FOIA corpus, and the two
  applications stay separate.
- Anything from Vivarium's physical-object apparatus: image pipeline, condition,
  provenance, inscription, shelf location, the three-tier visibility system.
- Notes in Vivarium proper. Recommended there on their own terms, and tracked
  separately from this project.

## 8. Decisions

Resolved items are marked and kept in place; the numbering does not change, so
cross-references elsewhere in this document still resolve. The exam date is
settled: **the exam date**.

1. **The split between text-layer PDFs and scans.** Now the blocking
   measurement, because it decides whether F-1 is reachable before the exam. A
   corpus that is 80 percent text-layer is a weekend; one that is 60 percent
   scans is several weeks of OCR and quality checking. A triage pass over the
   files she holds, not a coding task. Do this first.
2. **Hosting — RESOLVED 0.5.** Neon serverless Postgres with pgvector, and
   extracted pages in Cloudflare R2 rather than in the database. Reasons, in
   order of weight: Supabase's free tier pauses a project after seven days of
   inactivity and it stays unreachable until someone opens the dashboard, which
   for a reader who works in bursts over two years means her notes go dark while
   she is at a conference; the standard remedy is a scheduled ping whose only
   purpose is defeating a policy. Neon suspends after five minutes and wakes
   itself on the next query in a few hundred milliseconds. Both free tiers cap at
   0.5 GB, but overrunning Neon costs cents per month against a $25 step at
   Supabase. Keeping pages in R2 follows the existing division — pages are the
   durable artifact, the database is derived — and removes about 70 MB from the
   cap. Cloudflare D1 with Vectorize was considered for its 5 GB ceiling and
   rejected: it would replace the whole Next-on-Vercel stack, and its full-text
   search support is unconfirmed, which would put §4.4 at risk.
3. **How many of the ~85 she actually holds digitally**, and which are missing.
   The handoff says about a hundred; the list is 80 bullets and the correspondence
   between the two has not been checked.
4. **Position card schema.** To be defined with her, letting the exam structure
   dictate it, before any card is generated. Blocks F-1. Separately **RESOLVED
   0.5**: cards are generated by hand through her own paid Claude subscription —
   a book's extracted text pasted into a conversation, the card reviewed there,
   the reviewed version entered into the application. No metered API, and the
   manual step is the review F-2 requires anyway.
5. **Embedding model.** Must be multilingual. The choice fixes the vector
   dimension in the schema. Not urgent; nothing in the comps slice touches it.
   Anthropic offers no embedding model, so this introduces the only third-party
   key in the system, or is avoided entirely by embedding locally at ingest and
   finding a free route for the runtime query embedding.
6. **Who operates it**, and whether she needs a browser interface or a
   conversation with the assistant is the whole interface.
7. **Whether notes must interoperate with her existing reference manager.** Not
   yet asked.
8. **Authentication for the web application.** Leaving Supabase removes Supabase
   Auth, which was the one piece of Vivarium's scaffold that would have
   transferred unchanged. Candidates: Neon's managed auth, a magic-link
   implementation, or something deliberately smaller given that the application
   has two users. Blocks nothing until the catalogue is exposed beyond localhost.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

Checkable, and each one stands for a class of error that shows up in this kind
of pipeline.

1. For ten spot-checked books, a printed page number returned by search matches
   the folio printed on that page of the source file.
2. A quotation retyped with two wrong words returns the correct book and page.
3. An accented Spanish phrase typed without accents returns the accented text.
4. An English query returns a relevant Spanish passage from a book with no
   English in it.
5. A search for a book's own title does not return three hundred hits from its
   running headers.
6. `curanderismo`, hyphenated across a line break in the source, is returned by a
   search for `curanderismo`.
7. Re-chunking a book leaves every note on that book anchored and retrievable.
8. A note written against a book before extraction is anchored and retrievable
   after that book is extracted.
9. Every note in an export states whether she wrote it, edited it, or has not yet
   read it.
10. No index or table of contents appears in semantic retrieval results.
11. Deleting and re-ingesting one book changes nothing about any other book.
